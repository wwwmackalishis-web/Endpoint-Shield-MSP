## Notes on what this script does and doesn't claim

Three lines here were written carefully. Don't loosen them without checking the claim still holds.

**"Checks in with us — automatically, around the clock."** True: agents report continuously and the platform records continuously. Note what the script does *not* say — that a person is watching at 3 a.m. The on-screen caption pairs with it: *"Reviewed and escalated during your service hours."* If you later staff overnight escalation, you can strengthen this. Until then, this wording is accurate and the stronger version is not.

**"It's flagged, and it's on the list to be reviewed."** Deliberately not "we call you immediately." Match this to the response targets in Schedule B of your BAA.

**"They're different questions."** This is the substantive point of the whole video and your best differentiator, because most competitors conflate the two. Your uptime endpoint and your §164.312(b) audit trail are separate systems doing separate jobs, and saying so out loud reads as expertise to anyone who has been through an audit.

**"We hold signed agreements with every provider behind us."** This is only true once you have executed BAAs with Render and any other infrastructure provider in the path. If those aren't signed yet, cut this clause from the read — the sentence works without it.

## Production notes

- **Music:** something warm and unhurried at low volume. Avoid the driving corporate-tech bed; this is a trust pitch, not a product launch.
- **Voice:** conversational, mid-pace. An office manager has been pitched at all week. Calm outperforms energetic here.
- **Captions are burned in**, so it plays silently on a website or in a waiting room. If you post it to social, that matters more than the audio.
- **Other cuts:** ask and I can render 1080×1080 (square, for LinkedIn/Instagram feed) or 1080×1920 (vertical), and a 20-second version that keeps only the BAA and audit-trail beats.
